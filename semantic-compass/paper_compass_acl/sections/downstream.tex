\section{Downstream behavior}
\label{sec:downstream}

We evaluate downstream behaviour on three benchmarks (WinoGender,
single-head StereoSet, held-out routed CrowS-Pairs), framing
compass rotation as a graded steering primitive with an
off-switch. The reported movements are surface-metric shifts, in
the sense of \citet{gonen2019lipstick}; whether they correspond to
the model encoding gender less or to a redirection of the same
signal is a question for a follow-up probe-recovery experiment.
Movement magnitudes should be read against the benchmark
annotation-noise floor reported by \citet{blodgett2021stereotyping}.

\subsection{WinoGender}
\label{sec:downstream:winogender}

We sweep $\alpha \in \{0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0\}$ on Phi-3 L28H1 and
report \emph{stereo corr}, the Pearson correlation between the
model's per-occupation
$\mathrm{logit}(\mathit{he}) - \mathrm{logit}(\mathit{she})$ and the
BLS female-occupancy rate; lower is less stereotypical, $0$ is
uncorrelated, negative is the opposite stereotype.

The compass intervention drives stereo corr through zero into
reverse-stereotypical territory at low perplexity cost
($+0.018$ at $\alpha = 1.5$, vs.\ $+0.139$ for ActAdd at a
comparable raw-gap shift). The reverse-stereotypical regime
($\alpha \geq 1.5$) is bias of opposite sign, not less bias; the
table shows the full sweep so the operating point is the user's
choice, and we make no claim that any single $\alpha$ is the right
one. A principled rule for picking $\alpha$ without a user-specified
target is open.

\subsection{CrowS-Pairs: held-out routed intervention}
\label{sec:downstream:crows}

For multi-domain CrowS-Pairs we use a routed ensemble: the top-4
heads passing the multi-domain scan in each bias domain, each
injected at SNR-calibrated $\alpha_d = \tau /
\overline{\mathrm{snr}}_h$ with target $\tau$ chosen per model
($0.20$ GPT-2, $0.08$ Phi-3 and Gemma, $0.10$ Llama).

\paragraph{The held-out procedure.}
We split CrowS-Pairs $50/50$ (seed 0) by domain. On the dev half we
sweep a per-cell multiplier $M \in \{0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0, 1.25,
1.5\}$ and pick $M^*$ minimizing $|\mathrm{SS} - 50|$ on the dev
half. We then report $\mathrm{SS}$ on the test half. Including
$M = 0$ in the sweep gives the procedure an off-switch when the
baseline is already neutral.

\paragraph{Three patterns.}

\emph{(1) Where the baseline is neutral, the procedure picks
$M^* = 0$.}
GPT-2 race ($M^* = 0$, test SS $= 50.00$); Gemma gender (test
$|\Delta_{50}| = 0.38$); Llama race ($2.71$); Llama gender
($3.44$). Direction-based projector baselines do not have this
property. Across the four-model panel $M^* = 0$ is selected on
$10$ of $16$ cells, and on $5$ of those $10$ the test-half
$|\Delta_{50}|$ is small ($<5$); on the other $5$ the dev sweep
found no helpful multiplier and the test half remains biased
(Phi-3 religion test $14.15$, Gemma profession $18.60$, Llama
religion $31.13$). $M^*{=}0$ is therefore an off-switch in the
former cases and a no-help signal in the latter.

\emph{(2) Where the baseline is biased, the compass moves it
modestly, and the natural between-split comparison is missing.}
On Phi-3 gender, the dev half $|\Delta_{50}|$ improves from $8.78$
at $M = 0$ to $0.38$ at $M^* = 0.50$; on the held-out test half the
same $M^* = 0.50$ lands at $|\Delta_{50}| = 5.73$. The
apples-to-apples comparison---test-half SS at $M = 0$ vs.\ test-half
SS at $M^* = 0.50$---requires running the M=0 condition on the test
half for cells where $M^* \neq 0$, which our experimental setup
did not do. The strongest defensible reading of the gender row is
therefore: the dev-selected $M^*$ generalizes onto the held-out half
at a smaller-but-non-zero distance from neutral. On GPT-2 gender,
$M^* = 0$ is selected because dev SS at $M = 0$ is already at
$51.91$ (within $2$ points of neutral). Even the dev-side movement
is well inside the \citeauthor{blodgett2021stereotyping}
annotation-noise band; we report it as suggestive rather than as
evidence of debiasing.

\emph{(3) Profession and religion overshoot the dev target on the
held-out side.}
Phi-3 profession test SS $= 60.47$ vs.\ baseline $57.56$;
Llama religion test SS $= 81.13$. These cells reveal genuine
train/test instability in CrowS-Pairs at small $n$; with religion
$n = 53$ the per-cell $95\%$ CI is roughly $\pm 13$ points, and
$M^*$ chosen on a $26$-row dev half is unreliable. We report these
cells to show the held-out procedure is not a guaranteed win.

\paragraph{What the held-out evaluation supports.}
The procedure --- per-domain head routing, SNR-calibrated $\alpha$,
dev-selected multiplier with a $0$-option --- is a methodological
improvement over fixed-projector baselines on three properties:
(i) it admits an off-switch when the baseline is already neutral;
(ii) it generalises across the dev/test split for the gender cell
on Phi-3 and the neutral cells on GPT-2 race, Llama gender, and
Gemma gender; (iii) it composes with per-domain calibration so
race / profession / religion can be steered independently.
Direction-based projector baselines on the same metric are in
\cref{app:projector_baselines}.
