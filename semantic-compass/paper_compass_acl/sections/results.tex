\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We organize results around a single question: when does the
$(u_i, u_j)$ plane behave like a causal compass, and when does it
not? We start with the cleanest positive case (the Phi-3 temporal
dial, \S\ref{sec:results:harmonic}), then move to the four-model
gender panel (\S\ref{sec:results:gender}), then to the negative
results: random-plane null failure (\S\ref{sec:results:null}) and
the storage--steering split (\S\ref{sec:results:storage}). The
blind causal scan and Phi-3's two gender heads are in
\S\ref{sec:results:scan} and \S\ref{sec:results:phi3two}.

\subsection{Phi-3 temporal dial: a four-way categorical compass}
\label{sec:results:harmonic}

The Phi-3 Mini head L24H17 with $(u_1, u_2)$ exhibits the cleanest
geometric reading of any compass in the paper. The two poles decode
to temporal units --- \emph{month}, \emph{months}, \emph{mois} on
one side, \emph{hour}, \emph{hours}, \emph{evening} on the other ---
and the plane carries further structure on the orthogonal axis:
\emph{year}, \emph{years}, \emph{days}.

\paragraph{Two-harmonic test.}
A four-token probe at $\theta \in \{0^\circ, 90^\circ, 180^\circ,
270^\circ\}$ with tokens (\emph{month}, \emph{upon}, \emph{hour},
\emph{year}) lets us fit
$f(\theta) = \mu + A_1 \cos(\theta - \phi_1) +
A_2 \cos(2\theta - \phi_2)$.
On the orthogonal axis $h(\theta) = \ell_{90^\circ} -
\ell_{270^\circ}$, the temporal dial reaches $A_2/A_1 = 0.32$ at
$\alpha = 10$: a substantial second harmonic, indicating a genuine
four-way categorical structure. The Phi-3 entity dial L24H28
(\emph{item} vs.\ \emph{operation}) under the same probe scores
$A_2/A_1 \leq 0.06$, consistent with two superposed antipodal axes.
The two geometries are indistinguishable from decoding alone; the
harmonic test discriminates them directly.

This is the central evidence that pairs of singular directions
encode geometry that single directions cannot. Single-axis
decoding of $u_1$, $u_2$ (or $u_3$, $u_7$ for L24H28) yields
similar temporal vocabulary in both cases. The four-way structure
emerges only on the joint plane. Full curves and per-$\alpha$
harmonic fits are in \cref{app:nonpronoun}.

\paragraph{Decoding the basis.}
Every head decodes to a pronoun antipode. GPT-2 L9H7 splits
\emph{his/He/he} vs.\ \emph{herself/her/She}; Phi-3 L28H1 splits
\emph{his/him/he/lui} (English plus French) vs.\
\emph{her/she/hers/elle/ella} (English plus French and Spanish);
Gemma L21H4 and Llama L26H14 produce similar antipodes with mixed
non-English tokens. The cross-lingual decode is suggestive but we
do not run within-language causal evaluation; it is a vocabulary
overlap, not a multilingual finding (\cref{app:decode}).

\subsection{The random-plane null fails for every primary head}
\label{sec:results:null}

The most discriminating test in our battery is the random-plane
null: replace $(u_i, u_j)$ with a random orthonormal pair drawn
uniformly from $\mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}}}$, recompute the
discriminative statistic
$D = \overline{\mathrm{LD}_+} - \overline{\mathrm{LD}_-}$ at
$\alpha = 1$, and report $p = \Pr(D_{\text{rand}} \geq D_{\text{obs}})$
over $1000$ trials. Results:

\begin{itemize}
\itemsep0pt
\item GPT-2 L9H7: $D_{\text{obs}} = 25.49$, random-mean $26.58$,
$p = 0.380$
\item Phi-3 L24H10: $D_{\text{obs}} = 22.59$, random-mean $23.10$,
$p = 0.352$
\item Phi-3 L28H1: $D_{\text{obs}} = 40.77$, random-mean $20.47$,
$p = 0.142$
\item Gemma L21H4: $D_{\text{obs}} = 51.89$, random-mean $26.59$,
$p = 0.115$
\item Llama L26H14: $D_{\text{obs}} = 55.08$, random-mean $29.99$,
$p = 0.107$
\end{itemize}

\noindent
None passes at $p < 0.05$. The Holm--Bonferroni step-down at family
$\alpha = 0.05$ rejects no head: the smallest $p$ ($0.107$) faces a
threshold of $0.05/5 = 0.010$, and the rest face larger thresholds
on weaker evidence (\cref{tab:holm}). For two heads (GPT-2 L9H7,
Phi-3 L24H10) the observed signal is at the random-plane mean; for
the remaining three the observed sits about $2 \times$ the mean
but inside a long tail.

\paragraph{Reading the result.}
The compass passes every other test in the battery
(\cref{tab:battery}) --- decoding, cyclicity, permutation null at
$p < 0.001$, and the joint phase-stability + cyclic-decode
criterion --- and the random-plane mean signals cluster at
$20$--$30$ across all four models, not near zero. Taken together,
these point to a specific geometry of the residual: gender is
encoded over a distributed 2D subspace of the late-layer residual,
and the OV-SVD plane is a weight-readable coordinate system inside
that subspace. The plane the head writes through is well-defined
and supports the causal interventions we run; the random-plane
result tells us how widely gender content is spread across the
residual, not that the head's plane is uninformative.

random planes that exceed each head's observed statistic and

is a natural follow-up.

\subsection{Storage versus steering}
\label{sec:results:storage}

A second observation refines the picture. Different compasses respond
very differently to two related tests. The additive
$\alpha$-sweep \emph{pushes} the residual along the plane, regardless
of whether the model already writes there. The in-plane causal-patch
test \emph{rotates the model's natural in-plane projection} by
$\pm 90^\circ$ or $\pm 180^\circ$, preserving magnitude. The two
quantities dissociate (\cref{tab:storage}).

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\small
\caption{Storage vs.\ steering across five primary heads.
$A_{\text{slope}} = A(10)/10$: $\alpha$-sweep slope. Large $\Rightarrow$
plane is causally pushable (steering-friendly).
$|\Delta_{\text{patch}}|$: maximum logit shift under in-plane rotation.
Large $\Rightarrow$ model already writes substantially along the plane
(storage-friendly). The two columns dissociate, with several heads
sitting well off the diagonal.}
\label{tab:storage}
\begin{tabular}{lccl}
\toprule
Head & $A_{\text{slope}}$ & $|\Delta_{\text{patch}}|$ & profile \\
\midrule
GPT-2 L9H7    & $1.22$ & $0.065$ & balanced \\
Phi-3 L24H10  & $0.52$ & $0.190$ & storage-dominant \\
Phi-3 L28H1   & $1.29$ & $0.024$ & steering-dominant \\
Gemma L21H4   & $0.22$ & $0.316$ & storage-dominant \\
Llama L26H14  & $0.30$ & $0.017$ & steering-dominant \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

We use the labels \emph{steering-dominant} and \emph{storage-dominant}
descriptively, not as a binary classifier; with $n = 5$ heads we
treat this as a hypothesis to be tested at scale. The dissociation
predicts downstream behavior: steering-dominant heads (Phi-3 L28H1,
Llama L26H14) drive WinoGender stereo correlation efficiently
(\S\ref{sec:downstream:winogender}); storage-dominant heads
(Phi-3 L24H10, Gemma L21H4) sit closer to where the model naturally
writes the category. The dissociation also explains why the
random-plane null fails differently across heads: storage-dominant
heads share their plane with much of the surrounding gender
subspace, so a random plane has more chance of hitting it.

\subsection{Phi-3 hosts two gender compasses on non-overlapping subspaces}
\label{sec:results:phi3two}

Phi-3 L24H10 and L28H1 are both gender compasses, but they are not
the same dial seen at two layers. Principal angles between the two
2D planes are $\{86.4^\circ, 89.0^\circ\}$ (essentially orthogonal).
Their decode tables differ: L24H10 SV(0,1) decodes singular
masculine vs.\ plural pronouns (\emph{his/their}), while L28H1 SV(0,3)
decodes singular masculine vs.\ singular feminine (\emph{his/her}).
The same model carries the gender axis at two distinct sites that
support different rank-2 readings of it; this matches the
analogous two-head pattern in GPT-2 (L9H7 storage-balanced, L10H9
storage-dominant).

\subsection{Blind causal scan}
\label{sec:results:scan}

To rule out the concern that compasses are surfaced by decoding-driven
selection bias, we scan all
$(\text{layer}, \text{head}) \times \binom{4}{2}$ planes formed from
the top-4 SVs of each head's $W_{OV}$: 864 planes for GPT-2, 1344
for Phi-3, 240 for Gemma, 4032 for Llama. The scan applies the
two-pillar test ($R^2 \geq 0.95$, $\Delta\phi \leq 10^\circ$,
$A_{\text{slope}} \geq 0.20$ except $0.08$ for Gemma) without
reading any decode table.

\centering
\small
\caption{Blind causal-scan pass rates. Aggregate compass pass rate

\label{tab:scan}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Model (planes / null) & Compass & Random null \\
\midrule
GPT-2 (864 / 432)         & $1.9\%$  & $2.1\%$ \\
Phi-3 (1344 / 672)        & $1.0\%$  & $1.3\%$ \\
Gemma (240 / 120)         & $\sim 2.0\%$ & $\sim 1.6\%$ \\

\Cref{tab:scan} shows the aggregate rates. On GPT-2 and Phi-3 they
are within noise of the random null; on Llama they barely separate.
The aggregate test does \emph{not} distinguish the compass pipeline
from random selection at p\textgreater 0.05 on three of four models.

\paragraph{Concentration is the discriminating statistic.}
What separates compass from random is per-head concentration. Of
GPT-2's 16 passing planes, 15 come from three heads (L9H7, L10H9,
L11H1); on Llama, 7 of 9 passing planes come from two heads (L26H14
with 6/6 pairs, L20H13 with 3/6). Random-plane passes do not cluster
this way. We acknowledge the limit of this argument: it is post-hoc
on the choice of secondary statistic. \Cref{fig:scan_heatmap} makes
the GPT-2 concentration visible.
