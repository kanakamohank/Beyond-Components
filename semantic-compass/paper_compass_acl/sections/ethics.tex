The compass primitive is dual-use: the same rank-2 rotation that
moves WinoGender stereo correlation toward zero, applied at the
opposite pole, moves it the other way. The intervention is also
asymmetric (\cref{tab:storage}): on GPT-2, masculine-coded
directions steer more strongly than feminine-coded ones, so a
practitioner applying the same $\alpha$ symmetrically will overshoot
in one direction and undershoot in the other.

\paragraph{Restricted scope.}
Downstream evaluation uses public benchmarks (Gender Pronoun,
WinoGender, intra-sentence StereoSet, CrowS-Pairs). These do not
generalise to deployment-style settings (long-form generation, RLHF
reward modelling, agentic systems); surface SS movement is a weak
indicator of deployed model behaviour. Practitioners using the
compass for fairness purposes should run the
\citet{gonen2019lipstick} probe-recovery diagnostic before
reporting any debiasing outcome.

\paragraph{Binary framing.}
The compass ``gender'' axis we measure is operationalised through
binary English pronouns. Deployment to systems serving non-binary
users, or to non-English language settings, requires
within-population evaluation that we do not provide.

\paragraph{Release.}
We release scan logs, calibration JSONs, per-head battery results,
and pinned commit hashes sufficient to reproduce every number in
\cref{tab:gender,tab:battery,tab:holm,tab:storage,tab:scan,tab:winogender,tab:stereoset,tab:crows_holdout}.
We do not release pre-computed steering tensors. The
$(\text{model}, \text{layer}, \text{head}, \mathrm{SV}\text{-pair})$
tuples in \cref{tab:gender} are sufficient to reconstruct the
intervention from public weights; the methodology is in the paper
rather than gated on an artefact.
